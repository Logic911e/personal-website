# First-Person 3D Bedroom Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Replace the 2D Phaser game with a first-person, Minecraft-style 3D bedroom where each project is a pinned note on a pinboard.

**Architecture:** Pure, unit-tested modules in `src/lib/` (collision, room layout data, look and movement maths, board note layout, mute rules, card HTML) feed a thin Three.js layer in `src/game3d/` and DOM glue in `src/main.js`. Walking is 2D collision in the floor plane, so the existing `stepPlayer` and `collides` are reused (rect `x` = world x, rect `y` = world z). The room is one editable data file. The 2D game stays working until Task 8 swaps the entry point; Task 9 deletes it.

**Tech Stack:** Three.js, Vite, Vitest, plain JavaScript (ES modules). Existing: `src/lib/projects.js`, `projectHtml.js`, `mute.js`, `viewport.js`, `movement.js`.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-08-3d-bedroom-design.md`. Also see `2026-11-08-bedroom-portfolio-design.md` for what carries over.

## Global Constraints

- Three.js replaces Phaser in the shipped site. No physics engine.
- Room interior: **3.5 m wide (x), 5.5 m long (z), 2.4 m high (y)**. One block is **0.25 m**. Plan view: back (bed, window) wall at `z = 0`, door wall at `z = 5.5`, left wall `x = 0` (board), right wall `x = 3.5` (desk). The visitor spawns just inside the door **facing the back wall** (looking toward -z), so the sketch's left is the visitor's left.
- Camera: eye height **1.6 m**, field of view **75 degrees**, walking speed **3 m/s**. No jumping, no head bob, no camera shake. Player footprint 0.5 m by 0.5 m.
- Controls: mouse look via pointer lock (the Enter click starts it), WASD and arrow keys walk, **E** uses the note in reach, **Esc** closes the card.
- Board: about **1.5 m long and 1 m tall**, centre **1.4 m** above the floor, flat on the left wall. One note per project, **0.28 m by 0.2 m**, at most **20** notes (5 across, 4 down); extra projects collapse into one final "See all projects" note linking to `fallback.html`; an empty list shows one blank note reading "No projects yet".
- A note is in reach when the player is within **1.5 m** and the crosshair is on it. Reach and aim use a ray from the camera.
- Card: HTML overlay with title, description, tags and an "Open project" link (`target="_blank"`, `rel="noopener noreferrer"`); http and https links only (existing `parseProjects` rule).
- Opening the card releases the mouse. Closing it (button or Esc) shows a "Click to resume" state. The same state shows if the lock is lost while exploring. If pointer lock is unsupported or refused, show a message with a link to `fallback.html`.
- Door is closed and solid. Spawn is clear of the closet.
- `?game=1` still forces the game on a narrow window. On a touch-only device it shows a "needs a keyboard and mouse" message with a fallback link. No WebGL: redirect to `fallback.html`.
- Music: looping song starts on the Enter click; Sound button mutes and remembers the choice; music also mutes while the tab is hidden **without changing the saved choice**.
- The room (layout, colours, furniture boxes) lives in `src/data/room.json`; collision boxes come from the same data.
- Look: flat-coloured blocks, simple lighting, no image textures except the canvas text on notes. Left and right walls pine (stripe pattern), back wall white, dark grey carpet, sage-green bed, grey curtains over a window above the bed, white desk with monitor and keyboard, white shelf unit with cubbies above the desk, dark blue-black chair, bass hung on the left wall by the bed, guitar on a stand beside the desk, closet by the door. No small clutter.
- Keep first-load light: nothing heavy beyond Three.js. Report the built bundle size at the end.
- Unit tests for pure logic; 3D rendering and DOM glue are verified by hand in a browser.

## Review Focus

1. Walls enclose the room: walking in any direction for a long time never leaves the 3.5 by 5.5 m interior (Task 2).
2. Looking straight up or down must not flip or spin the camera: pitch is clamped (Task 3).
3. A project title with HTML, a very long title, or emoji must not break the card or the note label: card text is escaped and labels are cut by whole characters, not bytes (Tasks 4 and 5).
4. Losing pointer lock (Esc, alt-tab, opening the card) must not leave a movement key stuck "down" (Task 7).
5. The tab being hidden and shown again must not overwrite the visitor's saved mute choice (Task 5).
6. Zero projects, exactly 20, and more than 20 projects lay out sensibly (Task 4).

Adjust to keep gaps.

### Task 3: Look and walk-direction maths

**Files:**
- Create: `src/lib/look.js`, `tests/look.test.js`

**Interfaces:**
- Produces:
  - `applyLook(state: {yaw: number, pitch: number}, dx: number, dy: number, sensitivity = 0.002): {yaw: number, pitch: number}`. Mouse right (`dx > 0`) turns the view right (yaw decreases); mouse down (`dy > 0`) looks down. Pitch is clamped to ±85 degrees (in radians). Yaw is wrapped into `[-π, π]`.
  - `moveDir(keys: {forward: boolean, back: boolean, left: boolean, right: boolean}, yaw: number): {x: number, y: number}`. Returns the plan-view direction (`y` is world z) for `stepPlayer`. At yaw 0, forward is `{x: 0, y: -1}`, right is `{x: 1, y: 0}`. Opposing keys cancel. The vector is rotated by yaw and is not normalised (`stepPlayer` normalises).

- [ ] **Step 1:** Write failing tests: yaw 0 forward gives `(0, -1)`; yaw `π/2` (turned left) forward gives `(-1, 0)`; opposing keys give `(0, 0)`; `applyLook` clamps pitch at 85 degrees after a huge `dy` and after a huge negative `dy` (Review Focus 2); yaw wraps after large `dx`; zero movement returns the same state.
- [ ] **Step 2:** Run; expected FAIL. Implement both functions. Run; expected PASS.
- [ ] **Step 3:** Commit `feat: look and walk-direction maths`.

### Task 4: Board note layout, labels and aim picking

**Files:**
- Create: `src/lib/board.js`, `tests/board.test.js`

**Interfaces:**
- Produces:
  - `type NoteSlot = { index: number, kind: 'project' | 'more' | 'empty', u: number, v: number, w: number, h: number, tilt: number }` (`u`, `v` in metres from the board centre, right and up; `w` 0.28, `h` 0.2; `tilt` in radians, within ±0.06, fixed and derived from `index`, not random).
  - `layoutNotes(count: number): NoteSlot[]`. `count 0` gives one `empty` slot. `1..20` gives `count` `project` slots filled row by row from the top left (5 across, 4 down). `count > 20` gives 19 `project` slots plus one `more` slot as the 20th. No two slots overlap and all fit inside the 1.5 by 1 m board.
  - `noteLabel(title: string, max = 18): string`. Returns the title if it has `max` or fewer characters, otherwise the first `max - 1` characters plus `…`. Counts whole characters (code points), never splits a surrogate pair.
  - `pickNote(hits: {distance: number, noteIndex: number}[], reach = 1.5): number | null`. Returns the `noteIndex` of the nearest hit within `reach`, else `null`.

- [ ] **Step 1:** Write failing tests: `layoutNotes(0)` is one `empty` slot; `layoutNotes(3)` is three `project` slots with distinct `u`/`v`; `layoutNotes(20)` is 20 `project` slots, none overlapping; `layoutNotes(21)` is 19 `project` plus one `more`; all slots inside board bounds; tilts within ±0.06 and identical across two calls; `noteLabel` leaves short titles alone, cuts a long one to 18 characters ending in `…`, and does not break an emoji at the cut (Review Focus 3); `pickNote` returns null for no hits, null when the nearest is 2 m away, the nearest index when two are in reach, and ignores a hit beyond reach even if first in the list (Review Focus 6).
- [ ] **Step 2:** Run; expected FAIL. Implement. Run; expected PASS.
- [ ] **Step 3:** Commit `feat: board note layout, labels and aim picking`.
